ENGLISH GRAMMAR QUIZ BANK
60 questions across six topics

Instructions: Choose the best answer. Each question includes the correct answer and a reason each other option is incorrect. Some questions test the most natural choice in the stated context.

============================================================
1. EVERYDAY TENSES: ROUTINES VS. RIGHT NOW
============================================================

1. Maya usually ___ the bus to work, but today she is walking.
A) takes  B) is taking  C) take
Answer: A) takes
Why: B describes an action happening now; the sentence describes her usual routine. C does not agree with singular subject “Maya.”

2. Be quiet! The baby ___ right now.
A) sleeps  B) is sleeping  C) sleep
Answer: B) is sleeping
Why: A is the simple present and usually describes a habit, not an action happening at this moment. C does not agree with singular “the baby.”

3. ___ your brother ___ in the city every day?
A) Is / working  B) Does / work  C) Do / works
Answer: B) Does / work
Why: A asks about an action in progress, not a daily routine. C uses “do” with a singular subject and incorrectly adds -s after the auxiliary.

4. Look! The children ___ a snowman.
A) build  B) builds  C) are building
Answer: C) are building
Why: A suggests a general activity or routine, not what is happening now. B also has incorrect agreement and does not express an action in progress.

5. I ___ coffee, so I usually drink tea.
A) am not liking  B) don’t like  C) doesn’t like
Answer: B) don’t like
Why: A stative verb “like” is normally used in the simple present for a general preference. C uses “doesn’t” with “I”; the correct negative auxiliary is “don’t.”

6. Why ___ you ___ my jacket? It’s cold outside!
A) do / wear  B) are / wearing  C) is / wearing
Answer: B) are / wearing
Why: A asks about a habit, but the situation points to what the person has on now. C uses “is” with “you”; use “are.”

7. Our English class ___ at 9:00 every Monday.
A) starts  B) is starting  C) start
Answer: A) starts
Why: B can describe a temporary arrangement, but “every Monday” marks a regular schedule. C does not agree with singular “class.”

8. This week, I ___ with my aunt while my apartment is being painted.
A) stay  B) am staying  C) stays
Answer: B) am staying
Why: A usually describes a regular or permanent situation; “this week” is temporary. C does not agree with “I.”

9. Listen! Someone ___ at the door.
A) knocks  B) is knocking  C) knock
Answer: B) is knocking
Why: A describes a habit or repeated event rather than the sound happening now. C lacks the -s needed for a simple-present singular subject, and it does not indicate an ongoing action.

10. My parents ___ TV in the evening; they prefer reading.
A) aren’t usually watching  B) don’t usually watch  C) doesn’t usually watch
Answer: B) don’t usually watch
Why: A uses the continuous form for a usual preference; the simple present is natural here. C uses “doesn’t” with plural “parents”; use “don’t.”

2. POLITE REQUESTS & MODALS
============================================================

1. You want the menu at a café. What is the most polite request?
A) Give me the menu.  B) Could I have the menu, please?  C) I want menu now.
Answer: B) Could I have the menu, please?
Why: A is an order and may sound abrupt. C is blunt, omits “the,” and “now” can sound demanding.

2. Ask a colleague to open the window politely.
A) Would you mind opening the window?  B) Open the window.  C) You must open the window.
Answer: A) Would you mind opening the window?
Why: B is an unsoftened command. C expresses obligation rather than a courteous request.

3. You need to borrow a pen. What should you say?
A) Can I borrow your pen, please?  B) Give me your pen.  C) I take your pen, okay?
Answer: A) Can I borrow your pen, please?
Why: B sounds like a command. C assumes permission and uses an unnatural phrasing; ask before taking it.

4. Ask a stranger for directions in a courteous way.
A) Tell me where the station is.  B) Could you tell me how to get to the station?  C) You know station?
Answer: B) Could you tell me how to get to the station?
Why: A is direct and can sound demanding. C is grammatically incomplete and may sound abrupt.

5. Your friend offers you tea. You would like some. What is a natural response?
A) Yes, please.  B) Give tea.  C) I demand tea.
Answer: A) Yes, please.
Why: B is a command rather than an acceptance. C is unnecessarily forceful and unnatural in this situation.

6. Ask your teacher for permission to leave early.
A) May I leave a little early today?  B) I leave early today.  C) I must leave early, teacher.
Answer: A) May I leave a little early today?
Why: B states an intention without asking permission. C sounds demanding and does not make a polite request.

7. You want someone to repeat a sentence. What is the most polite option?
A) Say that again!  B) Could you say that again, please?  C) You didn’t speak clearly.
Answer: B) Could you say that again, please?
Why: A is an abrupt command. C blames the speaker instead of politely asking for repetition.

8. Ask a hotel receptionist to call a taxi.
A) Would you be able to call a taxi for me?  B) Call taxi for me.  C) You will call a taxi.
Answer: A) Would you be able to call a taxi for me?
Why: B is a bare command and is missing “a.” C sounds like an instruction or assumption, not a request.

9. A coworker is carrying boxes. Offer help politely.
A) Shall I help you with those boxes?  B) You can’t carry those.  C) Give me the boxes.
Answer: A) Shall I help you with those boxes?
Why: B may sound critical and does not offer help directly. C is an order, not a courteous offer.

10. You want to use a friend’s phone briefly. What is the most polite request?
A) Could I use your phone for a moment?  B) I’m using your phone.  C) Hand over your phone.
Answer: A) Could I use your phone for a moment?
Why: B announces action without seeking consent. C is a forceful command.

3. PREPOSITIONS OF TIME & PLACE: IN, ON, AT
============================================================

1. The meeting begins ___ 3:00 PM ___ Monday.
A) at / on  B) in / at  C) on / in
Answer: A) at / on
Why: B uses “in” instead of “at” for a precise clock time and “at” instead of “on” for a day. C reverses the usual choices: use “at” for the time and “on” for the day.

2. Her birthday is ___ July.
A) at  B) in  C) on
Answer: B) in
Why: A is used for precise times or locations, not a month. C is used for a specific date or day, not a month by itself.

3. We have lunch ___ noon.
A) on  B) at  C) in
Answer: B) at
Why: A is used with days and dates. C is used with broader periods such as months, years, and parts of the day.

4. The picture is ___ the wall above the sofa.
A) on  B) at  C) in
Answer: A) on
Why: B is used for a point or specific place, not the surface supporting a picture. C suggests something inside the wall.

5. The keys are ___ my bag.
A) on  B) in  C) at
Answer: B) in
Why: A means on the bag’s surface. C marks a point or location and does not express that the keys are inside it.

6. Let’s meet ___ the entrance at 6:30.
A) at  B) in  C) on
Answer: A) at
Why: B would usually mean inside an entrance area and is not the usual way to name a meeting point. C is for a surface or a day/date.

7. The conference is ___ Friday morning.
A) at  B) on  C) in
Answer: B) on
Why: A is used with a precise time such as “at 9:00,” not a day plus part of day. C is used for “the morning” generally, but the specified day calls for “on.”

8. My grandparents moved here ___ 2018.
A) on  B) at  C) in
Answer: C) in
Why: A is used with a particular date or day. B is for precise times or named points, not a year.

9. The cat is sleeping ___ the table.
A) under  B) at  C) in
Answer: A) under
Why: B means at a location and does not describe the cat’s position relative to the table. C would mean inside the table, which is not the intended position.

10. There is a small café ___ the bank and the post office.
A) between  B) at  C) on
Answer: A) between
Why: B identifies a point but does not show the café’s position relative to two places. C describes a surface or a street/address context, not a position separating two places.

4. COUNTABLE & UNCOUNTABLE: MUCH, MANY, SOME, ANY
============================================================

1. Do you have ___ water? We need to fill this bottle.
A) many  B) any  C) a few
Answer: B) any
Why: A is used with plural countable nouns, but “water” is uncountable. C is also used with plural countable nouns, not “water.”

2. How ___ apples are left in the bowl?
A) much  B) many  C) any
Answer: B) many
Why: A is used with uncountable nouns; “apples” are countable and plural. C can be used before a noun in questions (“Are there any apples?”), but it cannot replace the quantity word in “How ___.”

3. There isn’t ___ milk in the fridge.
A) many  B) much  C) a few
Answer: B) much
Why: A is used with plural countable nouns. C is used with plural countable nouns and would require a positive amount, not this negative construction with uncountable “milk.”

4. We bought ___ oranges for the picnic.
A) some  B) much  C) any
Answer: A) some
Why: B is used with uncountable nouns, not plural countable “oranges.” C is common in questions and negatives; in this positive statement, “some” is the natural choice.

5. How ___ time do we have before the train leaves?
A) many  B) much  C) a few
Answer: B) much
Why: A is used with plural countable nouns; “time” here is uncountable. C requires a plural countable noun and would not fit “time.”

6. There are ___ chairs in the meeting room, so everyone can sit.
A) plenty of  B) much  C) any
Answer: A) plenty of
Why: B is used with uncountable nouns and does not fit plural “chairs.” C is not used to express an ample quantity in this positive statement.

7. Would you like ___ tea?
A) some  B) many  C) a few
Answer: A) some
Why: B is used with plural countable nouns, while “tea” is uncountable here. C also needs a plural countable noun.

8. There aren’t ___ clean plates in the cupboard.
A) much  B) many  C) a little
Answer: B) many
Why: A and C are used with uncountable nouns; “plates” are countable and plural.

9. I only need ___ information about the bus schedule.
A) a few  B) a little  C) many
Answer: B) a little
Why: “Information” is uncountable, so A and C, which go with plural countable nouns, are incorrect.

10. We don’t have ___ eggs, but we do have ___ flour.
A) any / some  B) much / many  C) some / many
Answer: A) any / some
Why: B reverses the forms: “eggs” are countable and “flour” is uncountable. C uses “some” in a negative clause where “any” is expected, and “many” does not fit uncountable “flour.”

5. COMMON CONFUSING WORD PAIRS
============================================================

1. Could you ___ me a favor?
A) make  B) do  C) give
Answer: B) do
Why: “Do someone a favor” is the standard expression. A is used in phrases like “make a decision,” not “make a favor.” C does not form the usual expression.

2. I need to ___ my teacher that I will be late.
A) say  B) tell  C) speak
Answer: B) tell
Why: “Tell” normally takes a person as its object (“tell my teacher”). A usually needs “to” before the listener or is followed by the words spoken. C is not used with a person in this pattern; say “speak to my teacher.”

3. Please ___ me your name so I can write it down.
A) tell  B) say  C) talk
Answer: A) tell
Why: “Tell me your name” is the standard pattern. B would need “say your name to me.” C usually takes “to/with” for the listener and does not fit this structure.

4. We watched the children ___ in the school play.
A) look  B) see  C) perform
Answer: C) perform
Why: A means direct your eyes toward something, not observe an event from beginning to end. B means notice or perceive, and does not express the sustained activity as clearly as “watched … perform.”

5. ___ at that beautiful rainbow!
A) Watch  B) Look  C) See
Answer: B) Look
Why: A is usually for something changing or continuing over time, such as a match. C describes perceiving something, not the usual command to direct your eyes toward it.

6. Could I ___ your charger for an hour and return it later?
A) lend  B) borrow  C) owe
Answer: B) borrow
Why: The speaker receives the charger temporarily, so the speaker borrows it. A means give something temporarily to someone else. C means have a debt and does not describe taking an item temporarily.

7. Could you ___ me your charger until tomorrow?
A) borrow  B) lend  C) owe
Answer: B) lend
Why: The listener is being asked to give the charger temporarily. A means receive something temporarily. C means be in debt and is unrelated to the request.

8. The new rule will ___ everyone in the office.
A) affect  B) effect  C) effort
Answer: A) affect
Why: “Affect” is the verb meaning influence. B is usually a noun meaning result, so “will effect everyone” is not the intended construction. C means an attempt or energy, not influence.

9. The medicine had a positive ___ on her sleep.
A) affect  B) effect  C) effort
Answer: B) effect
Why: “Effect” is the noun meaning result. A is usually a verb, and “a positive affect” does not fit this meaning. C means an attempt or energy, not a result.

10. We arrived ___ the airport just before noon.
A) to  B) at  C) in
Answer: B) at
Why: “Arrive at” is used with a specific place such as an airport. A is not used after “arrive” in this construction. C is more typical with cities or countries, not this specific destination.

6. QUESTION TAGS
============================================================

1. You like coffee, ___?
A) do you  B) don’t you  C) aren’t you
Answer: B) don’t you
Why: A repeats the positive polarity instead of using the usual negative tag after a positive statement. C uses “be,” but the statement’s main verb is “like,” so use “do.”

2. She isn’t coming tonight, ___?
A) is she  B) isn’t she  C) does she
Answer: A) is she
Why: B repeats the negative polarity; a negative statement normally takes a positive tag. C uses “does” instead of the auxiliary “is” in the statement.

3. They have finished the project, ___?
A) haven’t they  B) didn’t they  C) have they
Answer: A) haven’t they
Why: B uses past simple “did” instead of matching the present perfect auxiliary “have.” C repeats the positive polarity rather than switching to a negative tag.

4. Mark can swim, ___?
A) doesn’t he  B) can’t he  C) can he
Answer: B) can’t he
Why: A uses “does” instead of the modal “can.” C repeats the positive polarity; a positive statement normally takes a negative tag.

5. We should leave soon, ___?
A) shouldn’t we  B) don’t we  C) should we
Answer: A) shouldn’t we
Why: B uses “do” instead of matching the modal “should.” C repeats the positive polarity instead of using the usual negative tag.

6. Your parents live nearby, ___?
A) do they  B) don’t they  C) aren’t they
Answer: B) don’t they
Why: A uses a positive tag after a positive statement. C uses “be,” but the main verb is “live”; use “do.”

7. I’m late, ___?
A) am I  B) aren’t I  C) don’t I
Answer: B) aren’t I
Why: A repeats the positive polarity, while this special tag conventionally uses “aren’t I?” after “I am.” C uses the wrong auxiliary because the statement uses “am.”

8. Let’s take a break, ___?
A) will we  B) shall we  C) do we
Answer: B) shall we
Why: “Let’s” suggestions conventionally take “shall we?” A does not use the usual tag for a “let’s” suggestion. C uses “do” rather than the conventional tag.

9. Nobody called while I was out, ___?
A) did they  B) didn’t they  C) was it
Answer: A) did they
Why: “Nobody” makes the statement negative in meaning, so the tag is positive. B is negative and does not match the polarity. C uses the wrong auxiliary and pronoun; “nobody” is referred to as “they” in the tag.

10. There are two tickets left, ___?
A) aren’t there  B) are there  C) isn’t it
Answer: A) aren’t there
Why: A positive statement takes a negative tag and keeps the “there” construction. B is positive instead of negative. C uses the wrong pronoun and singular “is” for plural “two tickets.”
